Polymer Cable Cleats

A cable cleat has one job: hold the cable where it is during a short circuit. Our polymer range does that with a moulded, halogen-free, flame-retardant body instead of metal — electrically insulating, corrosion free and maintenance free. Two families cover the usual layouts: PCC-S for single-core cables from 13 to 140 mm and PCC-T for three-core cables laid in trefoil formation from 27 to 90 mm.

Key Advantages

The body is a moulded polymer compound, so the cleat works as an insulator as well as a clamp. That changes what it can do for an installation, compared with a metal cleat.

  • Halogen-free. Fluorine, chlorine, bromine and iodine were not detected in the compound (EN 14582, detection limit 50 mg/kg).
  • Flame retardant. Rated UL94 HB. No flame front reached the 25 mm or 100 mm mark in the horizontal burning test.
  • Short-circuit tested. Passed the short-circuit test to IEC 61914:2021 clause 9.5.3 and IEC 60502-4:2010: after the test the cable was still locked in position and undamaged.
  • Electrically insulating. The compound is an electrical insulator, so the cleat breaks the conductive path between the cable and the steelwork, and a non-metallic body removes the eddy-current and hysteresis heating that metal cleats can introduce in single-core AC circuits.
  • Corrosion free, zero maintenance. The polymer body cannot rust: no coating to renew, no rust staining on the tray. Bolts are stainless or galvanized.
  • UV and chemical resistant, rated for -55 °C to +105 °C.
  • Lightweight, with smooth rounded edges. Lower point load on cable trays and no sharp metal edge against the sheath.
  • Bolt options: 316 stainless, 304 stainless or galvanized steel, M10 / M12, 170 mm.

Short-Circuit Performance

The range has been short-circuit tested at an independent laboratory to IEC 61914:2021 clause 9.5.3 and IEC 60502-4:2010. In the test the cleats were mounted on a cable ladder, the cable run was restrained at five equally spaced positions, one end was connected to a three-phase supply and the other to a short-circuiting busbar with all three phases connected. The cleats withstood the test current, the cable stayed locked and was not damaged.

One cleat per cable, for single-core power, control and signal cables. Eight sizes cover cable outer diameters from 13 mm to 140 mm, so the same base cleat body suits a lighting final circuit and a 140 mm single-core feeder. PCC-T Trefoil Cable Cleat

One cleat holds all three cores of a three-core cable in trefoil formation. Four sizes cover 27 mm to 90 mm. The trefoil layout keeps the three phases in a balanced magnetic arrangement, and clamping them as one unit prevents the cores from separating when short-circuit forces push them apart.

Polymer or Stainless Steel?

We supply both. The polymer range is the better fit when the job needs electrical isolation, freedom from corrosion and a light cleat; stainless steel stays the choice for the heaviest mechanical duty. The table below is a straight comparison of the two ranges we make, not a claim that one replaces the other.

Choose polymer when
  • Single-core or trefoil LV/MV cables on trays, ladders and in tunnels
  • The installation needs isolation between cable and steelwork, or the client asks for a non-metallic cleat
  • Single-core AC circuits where eddy-current heating from metal cleats is a concern
  • Humid, coastal, chemical or washdown areas where rust staining and coating renewal are a nuisance
  • Weight matters, or the tray is already heavily loaded
  • Halogen-free material is specified for the cable accessories as well as the cable
Stay with stainless steel when
  • Very large cable mass or heavy mechanical impact is expected
  • Offshore topsides and marine projects where DNV type approval is a tender requirement
  • Continuous temperature above 105 °C
  • The specification names a metal cleat, or fire performance rules require it

Selection Steps

1
Measure the Cable OD

Take the actual outer diameter of the cable, not the nominal size. Each cleat size covers a fixed OD band in the tables above.

2
Choose Single or Trefoil

PCC-S for one cable per cleat; PCC-T when three cores are laid in trefoil formation and must be held as a group.

3
Confirm the Short-Circuit Duty

Give us the prospective short-circuit current and the cleat spacing from the project specification so we can confirm the size and the test coverage for your case.

4
Pick the Bolt Material

316 stainless for coastal and chemical sites, 304 for general indoor work, galvanized steel where the budget is tight and the environment is dry.
